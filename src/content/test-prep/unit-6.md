# Unit 6: Essay / Writing Sample

## Do You Even Need It?

> [!warning] Check First
> The SAT Essay and ACT Writing test are both **optional** and increasingly rare requirements — most colleges no longer ask for them. The SSAT Writing Sample, by contrast, is **always included** but is unscored; it's sent directly to schools for them to judge writing ability and "fit." Check your target schools/programs before spending prep time here.

---

## SSAT Writing Sample

- Upper Level: choice of a creative prompt or a persuasive-style prompt (varies by form)
- Not scored, but read by admissions — legibility, organization, and voice matter
- ~25 minutes, roughly one page

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> Admissions officers are looking for authentic voice and clear structure over "impressive" vocabulary. A well-organized, honest response beats an overwritten one with forced big words.

**Structure that works under time pressure:**
1. Hook / setup (1-2 sentences)
2. Body — specific details, not generalities
3. Brief reflection or conclusion tying back to the opening

> [!example] Worked Example: Weak vs Strong Opening
> Weak: "I am going to write about a time I was challenged."
> Strong: "The gym went silent the moment I missed the free throw that would have tied the game."
>
> The strong version drops the reader into a scene instead of announcing the topic.

## SAT / ACT Essay (if required)

Both ask you to analyze how an author builds an argument — **not** to give your own opinion on the topic.

> [!important] Analyze the Argument, Don't Just Agree/Disagree
> The #1 scoring mistake: writing about whether you *agree* with the passage's claim instead of *how the author persuades* the reader (evidence, reasoning, stylistic/persuasive elements).

**What graders look for:**
- Identify specific persuasive techniques (evidence, reasoning, emotional appeals, word choice)
- Support your analysis with specific examples/quotes from the passage
- Organize clearly: intro, 2-3 body paragraphs each on one technique, conclusion

> [!example] Worked Example: Technique Identification
> If a passage cites a scientific study, that's an appeal to **evidence/logic (logos)**. If it opens with a personal story about a struggling family, that's an appeal to **emotion (pathos)**. Name the technique explicitly in your essay rather than just describing what the author said.

---

## General Timed-Writing Strategy

1. **Spend the first ~5 minutes planning**, not writing. A quick outline prevents a rambling essay.
2. **Don't try to be clever with vocabulary** — clarity and specificity score higher than fancy words used incorrectly.
3. **Leave 2-3 minutes to proofread** for basic errors (missing words, unclear pronouns) — first drafts under time pressure often have small slips.

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> Practice writing to a clock at home before test day. Most students' biggest essay problem isn't ideas — it's running out of time mid-conclusion.
