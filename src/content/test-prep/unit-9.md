# Unit 9: Advanced Math (Geometry, Trig, Data)

## Geometry Essentials

**Angles:**
- Angles on a straight line sum to $180°$
- Angles around a point sum to $360°$
- Vertical angles (formed by two crossing lines) are equal
- Parallel lines cut by a transversal: corresponding angles equal, alternate interior angles equal

**Triangles:**
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}bh \qquad \text{Angles sum to } 180°$$

**Pythagorean theorem** (right triangles only):
$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> Memorize common Pythagorean triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17. Also their multiples (6-8-10, etc.) — they show up constantly and save calculation time.

**Circles:**
$$\text{Circumference} = 2\pi r \qquad \text{Area} = \pi r^2 \qquad \text{Arc length} = \frac{\theta}{360°} \times 2\pi r$$

**Volume formulas:**

| Shape | Volume |
|---|---|
| Rectangular box | $lwh$ |
| Cylinder | $\pi r^2 h$ |
| Cone | $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ |
| Sphere | $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ |

> [!warning] Common Mistake
> Confusing radius and diameter. If a problem gives you the diameter, halve it before plugging into area/circumference formulas.

## Right Triangle Trigonometry

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> Remember **SOH-CAH-TOA**. On the SAT, also know that $\sin(\theta) = \cos(90° - \theta)$ — complementary angles have swapped sine/cosine values.

> [!example] Worked Example
> A right triangle has legs 3 and 4, hypotenuse 5. Find $\sin\theta$ for the angle opposite the side of length 3.
>
> $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \dfrac{3}{5}$

## Coordinate Geometry

**Slope:**
$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

**Slope-intercept form:** $y = mx + b$ (slope $m$, y-intercept $b$)

**Distance between two points:**
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}$$

**Midpoint:**
$$\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$$

**Circle equation** (center $(h,k)$, radius $r$):
$$(x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2$$

## Statistics & Data Analysis

- **Mean** = sum ÷ count
- **Median** = middle value (sort first!)
- **Mode** = most frequent value
- **Range** = max − min

> [!warning] Common Mistake
> Forgetting to sort the data before finding the median. The median of {5, 1, 3} is 3 (sorted: 1, 3, 5), not the middle number as originally listed.

**Reading scatterplots/graphs:** identify the trend (positive, negative, no correlation) and watch for outliers that don't fit the pattern the question is asking about.

**Probability:**
$$P(\text{event}) = \frac{\text{favorable outcomes}}{\text{total outcomes}}$$

> [!example] Worked Example
> A bag has 4 red and 6 blue marbles. What's the probability of picking a red marble?
>
> $P(\text{red}) = \dfrac{4}{10} = 0.4$

## Strategy for Advanced Math

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> Draw a picture. Geometry and trig problems are much easier once labeled — mark known angles/sides directly on the figure (or sketch one if none is given).

> [!important] Know Your Formulas Cold
> The SAT provides a reference sheet with some formulas, but the ACT and SSAT mostly don't. Memorize area, volume, and Pythagorean formulas so you're not searching for them under time pressure.
