# Unit 10: Timing, Pacing & Test-Day Tactics

## Pacing Is Its Own Skill

> [!note] Key Idea
> Most missed points on these tests aren't from not knowing the material — they're from running out of time or rushing the last third of a section. Pacing has to be practiced separately from content.

**Rough per-question budgets:**

| Section | Time | Approx. per question |
|---|---|---|
| SAT Reading & Writing (per module) | 32 min / 27 Q | ~1.2 min |
| SAT Math (per module) | 35 min / 22 Q | ~1.6 min |
| ACT English | 45 min / 75 Q | ~0.6 min |
| ACT Math | 60 min / 60 Q | ~1 min |
| ACT Reading | 35 min / 40 Q | ~0.9 min |
| ACT Science | 35 min / 40 Q | ~0.9 min |
| SSAT Quantitative (each) | 30 min / 25 Q | ~1.2 min |
| SSAT Verbal | 30 min / 60 Q | ~0.5 min |
| SSAT Reading | 40 min / 40 Q | ~1 min |

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> These are averages, not per-question limits. Some questions take 15 seconds, some take 3 minutes — the budget is for the *section*, so bank time on easy questions to spend on hard ones.

---

## The "Two-Pass" Strategy

1. **First pass**: answer every question you can solve quickly and confidently. Skip (don't blank-guess yet) anything that stalls you for more than ~30-45 seconds.
2. **Second pass**: return to skipped questions with remaining time, using leftover minutes on the hardest ones.
3. **Final minute**: if time is about to run out, blind-guess remaining blanks (SAT/ACT — no penalty) rather than leaving them empty.

> [!warning] Common Mistake
> Getting stuck on question 8 for 4 minutes and never reaching questions 35-40, which might have been easier. A skipped easy question costs you more than a missed hard one.

## Test-Day Logistics

- **Sleep matters more than a last-minute review session.** A tired brain makes careless errors that no amount of extra content review fixes.
- **Eat something before the test** — a mid-test energy crash costs focus you can't get back.
- **Bring what's required**: photo ID, admission ticket, approved calculator with charged/fresh batteries, extra pencils, an analog watch if allowed (no smartwatches).
- **Know the room policies** — phones are usually banned even as a clock; bring a separate watch to track time yourself instead of relying on proctor announcements.

> [!important] Do a Dry Run
> Take at least one full-length practice test under real timed conditions (same time of day, same break lengths, no phone) before test day. The first time you experience the full length of the test should not be the actual exam.

---

## Managing Test Anxiety

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> If you blank on a question, don't spiral — skip it, keep momentum on questions you *can* answer, and come back. One hard question is not indicative of the rest of the section.

- Practice slow breathing (4 counts in, 4 counts hold, 4 counts out) if you feel panic rising mid-test — it takes 15 seconds and resets focus
- Positive self-talk framing helps more than it sounds: "I've prepared for this" beats spiraling on one tough question
- Familiarity reduces anxiety — the more real timed practice tests you've taken, the less novel (and scary) the real one feels

> [!example] Worked Example: Recovering Mid-Section
> You hit question 15 of 40 with 10 minutes left and panic. Stop, take 3 breaths, check your pace against the budget table above, and decide: skip-and-return on anything unclear, keep moving on anything solvable. Don't freeze on one question while the clock runs on all the others.
