# Unit 8: Algebra & Problem Solving

## Linear Equations & Inequalities

$$ax + b = c \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = \frac{c - b}{a}$$

- Solve inequalities the same way as equations, **except flip the sign when multiplying/dividing by a negative**.
- Systems of two linear equations: solve by substitution or elimination.

> [!warning] Common Mistake
> Forgetting to flip the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number. $-2x > 6 \Rightarrow x < -3$, not $x > -3$.

> [!example] Worked Example: System of Equations
> $2x + y = 10$ and $x - y = 2$. Add the equations: $3x = 12 \Rightarrow x = 4$. Substitute: $y = 2$.

## Ratios, Proportions, and Percent

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \quad \Rightarrow \quad ad = bc$$

**Percent change:**
$$\text{% change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100$$

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> "20% more than 50" means $50 \times 1.20 = 60$, not $50 + 20 = 70$. Convert percent language into a multiplier before calculating.

> [!example] Worked Example
> A shirt costs $40 after a 20% discount. What was the original price?
>
> Discounted price = 80% of original: $0.80x = 40 \Rightarrow x = 50$.

## Exponents & Radicals

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| $x^a \cdot x^b = x^{a+b}$ | $x^2 \cdot x^3 = x^5$ |
| $\dfrac{x^a}{x^b} = x^{a-b}$ | $\dfrac{x^5}{x^2} = x^3$ |
| $(x^a)^b = x^{ab}$ | $(x^2)^3 = x^6$ |
| $x^{-a} = \dfrac{1}{x^a}$ | $x^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{x^2}$ |
| $x^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{x}$ | $x^{1/2} = \sqrt{x}$ |

## Quadratics

**Factoring:** find two numbers that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$ in $x^2 + bx + c$.

**Quadratic formula:**
$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

> [!example] Worked Example
> Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$. Need two numbers multiplying to 6, adding to −5: −2 and −3.
>
> $(x-2)(x-3) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2 \text{ or } 3$.

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> If a quadratic doesn't factor cleanly, don't waste time guessing — jump straight to the quadratic formula.

## Word Problems: Translating English to Math

| Phrase | Math |
|---|---|
| "is", "was", "equals" | = |
| "more than", "increased by" | + |
| "less than" (reverses order!) | − |
| "of" (with percent/fraction) | × |
| "per", "for every" | ÷ or rate |

> [!warning] Common Mistake
> "5 less than x" means $x - 5$, **not** $5 - x$. "Less than" reverses the order the words appear in.

## Functions

$f(x)$ notation just means "plug the input into the rule for $f$."

> [!example] Worked Example
> If $f(x) = 2x^2 - 3$, find $f(-2)$.
>
> $f(-2) = 2(-2)^2 - 3 = 2(4) - 3 = 5$.

---

## Strategy: Plugging In Numbers & Answers

> [!tip] Exam Tip
> When a problem has variables in the question *and* the answer choices, pick simple numbers (like 2 or 10), solve the problem with them, and see which answer choice matches. This is often faster and safer than pure algebra.

> [!example] Worked Example: Plugging In
> "If $x$ widgets cost $y$ dollars, how many dollars do $z$ widgets cost?" Let $x = 2, y = 10$ (so each widget = $5), $z = 3$ → answer should be 15. Test each answer choice with these numbers; the one that gives 15 is correct.
